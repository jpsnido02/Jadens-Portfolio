# Case study template

A block vocabulary and four story shapes, so seven case studies share a spine
without reading as one filled-in form.

Derived from measurements of five reference pieces (type scales, media widths,
section counts and page heights all read off the live pages, not eyeballed).

---

## 1. What each reference actually does

### Plaid Passport — `bai.framer.website/plaid/passport`

3,219px tall. **Four** media blocks. Seventeen text blocks. That is the whole
case study.

- 900px column, flush left at 62px. The lede paragraph is the only thing
  indented (112px) — a single hanging indent that sets the abstract apart.
  (Noted but not adopted: everything on our pages shares one left edge.)
- Title at 72px against section labels at 19px/500. A 3.8× jump, and the
  labels are barely larger than body (20px/400) — they differ by **weight
  only**.
- Rhythm repeats exactly three times: label → paragraph → media.
- Media widths: a 444+444 pair, then 900, then 900.

**Steal:** the caption *"Interaction explorations, not final designs"*, and the
ending — *"Check in later to see the final result 👀"*. It ships an unfinished
case study and says so. Removes the pressure to wait for a project to be
"done", which is why portfolios go stale.

### TikTok News — `yilinniu.io/works/tiktok-news`

14,398px tall. Fifteen media blocks. The opposite extreme, but structurally
the smartest of the five.

- Spec-sheet header: `TEAM / SCOPE / DATE / DOMAIN`, then `ABOUT` and
  `MY ROLE` as bullets.
- `◉` marks a major section, `◎` a sub-section. A glyph hierarchy, no numbers.
- Every section is **small label → 40px thesis sentence → body**. The theses
  alone read as a complete argument: *"News was becoming a strategic vertical
  at global scale" → "The ecosystem wasn't structured to support news at
  scale" → "How might we create a system where…" → "Build the system to
  support…" → "From strategy to implementation" → "Measure success."*
- Six features grouped into **three layers** (User Perception / Publisher
  Value / Platform Growth). That grouping is what turns a feature list into a
  system.
- Impact is three big numbers: +400% / +31% / +500%.

**Steal:** the thesis spine (a reader who only reads the big lines still gets
the story), the feature→layer grouping, and *"Some visuals are simplified or
conceptual to respect confidentiality"* — which is exactly the line Rokt work
needs.

### PearAI — `designbybryce.com/pear`

**One paragraph of prose in the entire case study.** Twelve media blocks.

- Persistent left rail listing all thirteen projects. You never return to an
  index; you switch.
- Content column pinned at 816px. Media heights alternate tall (547–551) and
  thin strip (181) — that alternation *is* the pacing.
- 249px square videos scattered at three different left offsets above the
  fold, like a collage rather than a grid.

**Steal:** the tall/thin alternation, and the nerve to let work speak with no
narration. Also the rail — cheaper to browse than back-and-forth.

### Kitchen Experiment — `alvinn.design/projects/kitchen-experiment`

Twenty media blocks across **six** width tiers (130 / 180 / 288 / 432 / 727 /
875). The best storytelling of the five, and the type scale is the most
restrained:

| | size |
|---|---|
| H1 | 24px |
| H2 | 20px — **identical to body** |
| H3 | 12px/700 caps |
| body | 20px/400 |
| caption | 14px |

One typeface. Seven size/weight combinations total. Hierarchy comes from
position and labelling, not scale.

Devices:

- **Title is a thesis, not a name:** "When touch is out of reach". The project
  name only appears in the URL.
- **Continuous figure numbering 1→10 across the whole piece**, like an academic
  paper. Ties every image to the prose and makes the page feel like one
  document instead of a stack of sections.
- **Documented failure, in sequence:** `ATTEMPT 1: WAVE TO SCRUB` → drawbacks
  → `ATTEMPT 2: DIAL TO SCRUB` → ergonomic problems → `3RD TIME'S THE CHARM:
  LEAN TO SCRUB` → why it won. Two dead ends shown before the answer.
- **The winning idea is traceable to an observation:** a study participant
  held down arrow keys to scrub YouTube. The solution is *earned*, not
  asserted.
- Section titles carry voice: "A sticky situation", "Ethnography study
  (definitely not a hoax to get participants to cook for us)".
- Three sections titled `Takeaway:` with opinionated subjects — "in praise of
  the barely working prototype", "ideas happen when you make things".
- One real anecdote doing more work than any metric: they kept delaying
  testing until they set a rule of *"no prototype, no dinner."*
- Ends with `Appendix: a fascinated dad` — his dad tried to scrub video with
  his head, and someone tried the interaction with a knife.

**Steal:** all of it. Specifically the failure sequence, the global figure
numbers, and ending on a human detail instead of a metric.

### Almaden Genomics — `yikodesign.com/#/almadengenomics`

Fifteen media, three width tiers (445 / 605 / 926). H1 29px, H2 22px, body
16px — six type combinations.

- `Project` metadata block: Duration / Role / Who I worked with / Problem Area.
- Key Insights are three short **noun phrases**, not sentences: "Tedious file
  organization prep before building", "Troubleshoot for errors".
- Process shown through real artifacts — the Mural board, four IA options
  sketched, diverge-then-converge.
- **Outcomes split in two:**
  - `Team's Success` — converted the first alpha partner to paying, secured
    IBM Ventures funding.
  - `Design Impact` — aligned the cross-functional team on product vision,
    showcased potential to IBM Ventures.

**Steal:** that split. It separates what the business got from what design
specifically contributed, which is the honest way to claim credit on work with
many hands on it.

## 2. Three findings that cut across all five

**Nobody writes "The Challenge / The Solution / The Outcome."** Every section
title in all five pieces is specific to that project. Generic headings are the
single clearest tell of a template.

**Four of five keep the type scale small and the hierarchy quiet.** Alvin's
section headings are the same pixel size as his body copy. Only Plaid goes big
(72px), and that is its one flourish on an otherwise tiny scale. Big section
headings are not what makes a case study feel designed — varied media widths
are.

**Media width variation is the actual pacing mechanism.** Three to six distinct
width tiers per page. A page where every image is the same width reads as a
document; a page with four width tiers reads as a layout.

## 3. The fixed spine

Four elements appear in every case study, so the set feels like one portfolio.
Everything else is optional.

1. **Masthead** — year, client, role, duration. Reuse the card palette: each
   case study inherits its project card's ink colour, so arriving from a yellow
   card lands you on a page tinted with that same yellow's ink. Free continuity.
2. **A one-paragraph abstract**, flush left like everything else, at a
   slightly larger size than body copy. Max 45 words.
3. **A thesis line** — one sentence, larger than anything else in the body,
   that states what the project argued. If it cannot be written, the case study
   is not ready.
4. **Onward navigation** — previous / next, previewing the next project's
   thesis rather than just its name.

## 4. Block library

The vocabulary. Each block is a component; a case study is an ordered list of
them.

| block | content | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `Masthead` | year · client · role · duration | fixed |
| `Abstract` | ≤45 words, one size up from body | fixed |
| `Thesis` | one sentence, large | fixed |
| `Credits` | named collaborators + their role | Alvin's generosity; good for Rokt team work |
| `Confidential` | one line on what is simplified or conceptual | needed for most Rokt work |
| `Figure` | media + numbered caption | numbering continuous across the page |
| `FigurePair` | two media side by side | for before/after and A/B |
| `FigureStrip` | thin wide media (≈1:4.5) | Bryce's rhythm-breaker |
| `Attempt` | label + media + what went wrong | the failure unit |
| `Insight` | short noun phrase + supporting media | Yiko's style — not a sentence |
| `Criteria` | 01/02/03 + one word + a question | TikTok's prioritisation block |
| `Layer` | groups 2–3 `Figure`s under a system name | turns features into architecture |
| `Quote` | pull quote + name + role | one per case study, maximum |
| `Numbers` | 2–3 metrics, large | only where real numbers exist |
| `SplitOutcome` | Business result / Design contribution | Yiko's split |
| `Takeaway` | opinionated title + short body | 2–3 per case study |
| `Anecdote` | one concrete human detail | the "no prototype, no dinner" slot |
| `WIP` | honest note that this is still moving | Plaid's ending |
| `Appendix` | the joke, the outtake, the weird one | optional, memorable |

## 5. Four story shapes

**The anti-copy-paste mechanism is not randomness — it is letting the project's
real story pick its structure.** Alvin's piece is built from attempts because
that is what happened. Bryce's is wordless because the work is visual. Pick the
shape that matches, and the structural variety is honest rather than decorative.

### Shape A — The Iteration
*For projects where the process was the story.*

`Masthead → Abstract → Thesis → Figure → Attempt → Attempt → Attempt(won) → Quote → Takeaway ×3 → Appendix`

The engine is two documented dead ends before the answer, and the answer traced
back to a specific observation. Highest trust-per-word of any shape.

### Shape B — The System
*For projects with a lot of surface area.*

`Masthead → Abstract → Thesis → Insight ×3 → Criteria → Layer → Layer → Layer → Numbers → WIP`

Group features into named layers. Three layers of two beats reads as
architecture; six ungrouped features read as a changelog.

### Shape C — The Gallery
*For visually strong or heavily confidential work.*

`Masthead → Abstract → Confidential → Figure(full) → FigureStrip → Figure(full) → FigurePair → FigureStrip → Figure(full) → Takeaway`

Under 80 words total. Captions do all narration. The tall/thin/tall alternation
is the entire pacing device.

### Shape D — The Reframe
*For projects whose value was changing the question.*

`Masthead → Abstract → Insight → Thesis(the reframe) → Figure → Figure → SplitOutcome → Takeaway ×2`

The turn is the content: what everyone assumed, the thing you found, the
different question that produced.

## 6. Variation levers

Even within one shape, vary these so no two pages open the same way:

- **Opening media width** — full bleed / contained / pair / thin strip. Four
  options, and it is the first thing a reader registers.
- **Title register** — a thesis ("When touch is out of reach") or a plain name
  ("Plaid Passport"). Alternate across the set.
- **Figure numbering** — on for Iteration and System shapes, off for Gallery.
- **Outcome treatment** — `Numbers` / `SplitOutcome` / `WIP`. Never the same
  one twice in a row in the card order.
- **Where the thesis sits** — before the first figure, or withheld until after
  it. Withholding builds a small amount of tension.

Rule of thumb: across seven case studies, no shape more than **twice**, and
never two of the same shape adjacent in the card order.

---

## 7. Word budget

Ruthless, because the stated goal is to show the work and not write about it.

| | words |
|---|---|
| Abstract | ≤45 |
| Thesis | ≤20, one sentence |
| Section body | ≤60 |
| Figure caption | ≤25 |
| Takeaway | ≤50 |
| **Whole case study** | **250–600** |

For comparison: Plaid's runs about 150 words, Alvin's about 1,400, TikTok's
well over 2,000. The target sits between the first two. Anything that pushes
past 600 wants to be a figure instead.

## 8. Implementation notes for this repo

The app is currently a single Vite page with no router — the project cards link
out via `data.link` in `src/content.ts`. Case studies need either:

- **a router** (`react-router`, ~15kB) with `/work/:slug` routes, or
- **separate pre-rendered pages** per case study, which keeps the bundle flat
  and the current `base: "./"` GitHub Pages setup working unchanged.

The second is probably right: case studies are documents, they do not need the
scroll engine, and they should be independently shareable links.

Either way the blocks in §4 are the component set, and each case study is a
data file listing its blocks in order — same authoring model as
`src/content.ts` today.

## 9. Shape assignment

Six projects, four shapes, in proposed card order. No shape repeats adjacently;
no shape is used more than twice. Outcome treatment and opening media width
also never repeat back to back, so no two pages open or close the same way.

| # | project | shape | title register | opens with | figures | ends with |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Macy's Bag page | **Iteration** | thesis | before/after pair | numbered | `Numbers` |
| 2 | Uber Eats Contextual Advertising | **Gallery** | plain name | full bleed | unnumbered | `Takeaway` |
| 3 | Branded Layouts | **System** | thesis | thin strip | numbered | `WIP` |
| 4 | Shoppable ad pitching | **Reframe** | plain name | contained | unnumbered | `SplitOutcome` |
| 5 | McDonald's kiosk | **Iteration** | thesis | full bleed | numbered | `Numbers` |
| 6 | Expedia 1P/3P | **System** | plain name | 1P/3P pair | numbered | `SplitOutcome` |

### Why each

**1. Macy's Bag page — Iteration.** Optimization work *is* the attempts. A bag
page has a narrow, measurable goal, so there will be variants that lost, and
losing variants are the most persuasive thing in the set. Leads the portfolio
because it is the one with real numbers attached. Three `Attempt` blocks, and
the winner traced back to a specific observation about the page.

**2. Uber Eats Contextual Advertising — Gallery.** Recognisable brand, visually strong output, and
almost certainly the most restricted. Gallery turns the confidentiality
constraint into the format rather than fighting it — under 80 words, captions
only, `Confidential` block up top. Second position because it is the one people
will recognise instantly while scrolling.

**3. Branded Layouts — System, ending on WIP.** One system applied across many
partners is exactly what `Layer` grouping is for: token extraction → templating
→ rollout, three layers of two beats. And since the metrics are still coming,
this is the Plaid move — ship it now with the honest note, add the numbers
later. Opening on a thin strip of many partner headers side by side states the
scale before a single word.

**4. Shoppable demo builder — Reframe.** The turn is that design was the
bottleneck: sales needed live demo prototypes for a new vertical, design
hand-built each one in Figma at about a week apiece, and the fix was removing
design from the loop rather than speeding it up. `SplitOutcome` earns its place
here — the business got self-serve demos, design got itself off the critical
path.

**5. McDonald's kiosk — Iteration.** The interesting content is the constraint
set, and it is unlike anything else here: a physical queue, no personal device,
seconds of dwell time, a stranger behind you who can see the screen. Formats
that work on a bag page will have failed outright, which gives real `Attempt`
blocks. Also the most memorable project in the set, so it earns a full-bleed
photograph of a kiosk in situ.

**6. Expedia 1P/3P — System.** First-party and third-party offers in one
experience is a structural problem with multiple surfaces, which is what
`Layer` handles. Opening on a 1P/3P pair puts the whole question in one image
before any prose.

### Open questions

- **Client naming.** Macy's, Uber, McDonald's and Expedia are all named partner
  relationships. Whether they can be named in public, and whether their UI can
  be shown, is a Rokt legal/partnerships question, not a design one — and it
  changes the structure, not just the copy. If names cannot be used, the shapes
  still hold but mastheads become category descriptors ("a national retailer",
  "a global QSR") and more case studies move toward Gallery.
